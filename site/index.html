The real problem

Most software projects don't fail on talent. They fail on process.

The way software gets designed, built and shipped grew organically — a brief, meetings, handoffs, revision loops — one workaround at a time. Nobody designed that process, and nobody sees it whole. That, not the technology, is what makes delivery slow and expensive.

The delivery process today

Knowledge trapped in people.

Every task waits on a calendar — a brief, emails, meetings, specialists, handoffs, revisions. The project moves as fast as its busiest person, and the knowledge walks out the door each evening.

  • → brief, then meetings
  • → a specialist for each step
  • → handoffs & revisions
  • → weeks, and a big invoice

The AI-native process

Knowledge in a legible context layer.

The brief, the brand, the conventions, the playbooks, the decision logs — written down well enough that agents can execute against them. People set direction and keep judgment; the fleet does the labor, in parallel.

  • ◆ humans own strategy & judgment
  • ◆ one legible context layer
  • ◆ a fleet of specialist agents
  • ◆ review at the gates, ship in days

The method

Make the process legible. Then hand it to agents.

The order is the whole trick. Automation applied to an undocumented process produces the same chaos, faster. So we fix the route before we accelerate it.

How it works

Four phases. You can stop after the first.

One orchestrator runs the pipeline — brief → design & build → deploy → grow & monitor. Agents execute; you sign off at the gates that matter.

01

Brief

A working session, then a written brief: scope, risks, acceptance tests — committed to the context layer. Stop here and the brief is still yours to keep.

02

Design & build

Specialists work in parallel: a real design system on the canvas, then code, payments, audits. You approve the design before it's built.

03

Deploy

Live on your infrastructure, behind a human go. The fleet runs on your machine — code and keys never leave it.

04

Grow & monitor

SEO, campaigns and a product film — then 24/7 monitoring with alerts on uptime, sales, traffic and security.

The gates

Where agents act alone — and where they never do.

reviewed after

Runs alone

Bounded, low-risk work: monitoring checks, report drafts, page generation. Done in the background, reviewed after the fact.

you direct

The productive middle: agents do the heavy lifting, you direct and approve. Design, builds and audits live here.

Under the hood

Built to be doubted.

You don't have to take the fleet's word for anything. The machinery assumes it will be questioned — and leaves evidence.

verification

Findings argue before they ship

Audit findings run a gauntlet: independent methodology passes, a fusion pass, then adversarial re-verification of every Critical, High and Medium finding. What survives is proven with a failing test.

confidentiality

Local-first by architecture

The fleet runs on your machine, inside your working tree. Deliverables are files you own; your code and keys never leave. There is no hosted platform to trust.

governance

Corrections survive

Every decision and correction is written into the context layer, with its provenance. The same mistake doesn't ship twice, and you can always see why something was done.
